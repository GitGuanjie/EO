Exchange-only quantum bits can be controlled using electrical signals rather than individually addressed magnetic fields, but their quantum operations often require long sequences of exchange pulses. Measuring a stabilizer for quantum error correction is a more specific task than implementing an unrestricted quantum gate. We show that two physical exchange pulses are unnecessary when the ancilla is prepared in a known state and measured immediately after the circuit. Using this observation, we shorten a nine-spin parity measurement while preserving its outcome-conditioned quantum action. Choosing a different, equally valid preparation path also reduces the simulated probability of reporting the wrong parity under an assumed correlated exchange-gain noise model. We test the construction on the full physical spin space and analyze a separate five-qubit, four-body reference with explicit connectivity assumptions. The calculations identify a practical compilation opportunity, but do not yet demonstrate repeated error correction or an experimental advantage.
